The problem

Launches miss when teams disagree

About a third of launches miss year-1 expectations, and most never catch up. Readiness is tracked as tasks completed and self-rated surveys. Nobody checks whether medical, access, sales and supply hold the same launch facts until a doctor or a hospital finds the gap.

36% of 149 drugs missed year-1 expectations; 70% of those still underperformed in years 2–3. Deloitte, 2020
What it does

One question bank, eight roles, five pulses

T-Minus turns the approved launch documents into short quizzes for each role and runs them from T-120 to T+90. It scores accuracy, confidence and how far functions disagree, then assigns micro-lessons where the gaps are.

“The first six months after launch are critical in determining the product's trajectory.” IQVIA, 2025
Why it can be trusted

Every question cites its source

Questions come only from approved documents and pass medical-legal review before release. The page blocks any generated question whose quote is not in the source text. Every result is logged per person as training and evaluation evidence.

China's Administrative Measures for Medical Representatives (in force 1 August 2026) require systems for rep training and evaluation.

Three demonstration launches across Sino's focus areas. Question banks and team results are illustrative, not Sino, CTTQ or GSK data.

Step 2

Take the pulse yourself

Answer six questions as a member of the launch team, and mark how sure you are. Confidently wrong answers are the ones that reach doctors.

Steps 3–4

Set the launch context, read the readiness

The whole launch team is simulated at the chosen pulse. Move any input and the scores, charts, fix list and brief update together.

Step 5

Gaps ranked by risk with an owner for each, a brief for the launch leader, and the compliance record. Hover a highlighted figure to find its row in the fix list.

Step 6

Build a question bank from your own document

Paste approved text. Claude drafts questions for a role, and the page checks each question's quote against your text before it can be used. The sample below is an illustrative launch truth sheet.

Methods

How the scores are made

Scores

Readiness index = 0.60 × accuracy + 0.25 × (100 − alignment gap) + 0.15 × (100 − 2 × confidently wrong %)

Accuracy is the share of answers that match the launch truth sheet. Confidently wrong is the share of answers that are wrong and marked Sure.

Alignment gap. For each question, the answer mix of every pair of functions is compared: the share of answers that would have to move for the two mixes to match. The gap is the average over all pairs, from 0 (every function answers alike) to 100 (no overlap).

Verdicts. An area is Go at 80% accuracy or more, a gap of 12 or less and 6% or less confidently wrong; Fix below 65% accuracy, above a gap of 22 or above 12% confidently wrong; otherwise Watch. The launch is Fix below an index of 65 or with two areas at Fix, and Go at 80 or more with no area at Fix, at most two at Watch and participation of 70% or more.

The simulated team

300 staff in eight functions (330 with the GSK team), each starting with an illustrative knowledge level per area. Training hours raise knowledge before each pulse. Micro-lessons add most where knowledge started lowest; without reinforcement, knowledge fades after launch. Individual ability varies around each function's level. Low leadership clarity lowers accuracy on positioning and objections, and makes each function's wrong answers cluster on a different option.
